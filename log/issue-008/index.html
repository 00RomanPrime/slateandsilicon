The Transition,
Not The Total.

The eighth transmission from The Dispatch. Two people can carry the same screen time and have completely different days. Here's the number that actually explains the difference.

Signal Eight

You can measure a day in screen time and learn almost nothing.

Two people can each carry three hours on a phone. One watched a film start to finish. The other opened six apps fifty times between meetings. Same number. Different day, entirely.

The number that actually matters isn't how long. It's how often something else got interrupted to make room for it.

There's a name worth using for that: fragmentation. Not "distraction," which blames the person. Not "addiction," which turns a Tuesday into a diagnosis. Fragmentation just means the thing you were doing didn't get to finish before something else took over.

A few kinds of transition, worth telling apart:

A chosen one. You finished the chapter, then checked messages.
A requested one. Someone needed an answer, so you gave it.
An automatic one. You opened the phone without remembering deciding to.
An avoidant one. The work got uncomfortable, so you found something easier.
An unclear one. You genuinely don't know why you picked it up.

None of those diagnose you. They just make something visible that's usually invisible.
